3I · Direct and inverse variation

Short key ideas and two tiny worked examples. Read one example, then try the same steps on the Exercise.

T Teacher note
Classroom wording is condensed from the student chapter. The gravel and travel examples use the same two rules as §3I: direct is y=kx, inverse is y=k/x. Exercise answers were recalculated from the questions. Printed slips in the worked-solutions PDF are listed once on the Exercise page.

Theory · key ideas

Find the constant from the pair you already know. Then the new value is one substitution.

  • Direct: y=kx. From a known pair, k=y/x. The line passes through (0,0).
  • Inverse: y=k/x. From a known pair, k=xy. The product stays fixed.
  • Same k: do not start again for the second question. Use the k you just found.
  • Double check: direct — double one, the other doubles. Inverse — double one, the other halves.
  • Other powers: y=kx^2, y=k/x^2, or y=k\sqrt{x} still have one k. Substitute the known pair into that formula.
y=kx   or   y=\dfrac{k}{x}

Worked mini-examples

Example · direct variation

Gravel is sold by the tonne. The cost C dollars varies directly with the mass m tonnes. Four tonnes cost $180. What do 10 tonnes cost?

Formula: C=km means cost varies directly with mass. Here k is the dollars charged for one tonne. From the given pair, k=C/m.

Step 1 · The given pair is C=180 when m=4.

Step 2 · Substitute that pair.

180=k\times 4

Step 3 · Divide both sides by 4.

k=\dfrac{180}{4}=45

So k=45 dollars per tonne, and the rule is C=45m.

Step 4 · New mass: m=10. Use the same k.

C=45\times 10=450

Ten tonnes cost $450. The navy dot is the given pair. The teal square is the new value. Both sit on the line through the origin.

Horizontal axis: mass m in tonnes. Vertical axis: cost C in dollars.

Example · inverse variation

A trip is 240 km. The time t hours varies inversely with the speed v km/h. At 60 km/h the trip takes 4 hours. How long does it take at 80 km/h?

Formula: t=k/v means time varies inversely with speed. Here k is the fixed distance, in kilometres. From the given pair, k=tv.

Step 1 · The given pair is t=4 when v=60.

Step 2 · Substitute that pair.

4=\dfrac{k}{60}

Step 3 · Multiply both sides by 60.

k=4\times 60=240

So k=240 and the rule is t=240/v.

Step 4 · New speed: v=80. Use the same k.

t=\dfrac{240}{80}=3

The trip takes 3 hours. Speed went up, so time went down. The navy dot is the given pair. The teal square is the new value.

Horizontal axis: speed v in km/h. Vertical axis: time t in hours. The labels sit off the curve.
