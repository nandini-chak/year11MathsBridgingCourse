3H · Two graphs that have asymptotes

Short key ideas and tiny worked examples. Read one example, then try the same steps on the Exercise.

T Teacher note
Classroom wording is condensed from the student chapter. The two families in this section are the exponential curve and the rectangular hyperbola. Exercise answers were checked against 3H-Worked-Solutions.pdf. Printed slips in that PDF are listed once, on the Exercise page.

Theory · key ideas

Two families. Each one has a line it flattens toward.

  • Exponential: y=a^x, base a>0 and a\neq 1. Three marks to show: y-intercept (0,1), the point (1,a), and the asymptote y=0.
  • Domain and range for y=a^x: every real x is allowed. Every output is positive: y>0.
  • Hyperbola: y=\dfrac{k}{x}. Two disconnected branches. No value at x=0.
  • Sign of k: k>0 puts the branches in quadrants 1 and 3. k<0 puts them in quadrants 2 and 4. Quadrant 1 is top-right, 2 is top-left, 3 is bottom-left, 4 is bottom-right.
  • Hyperbola asymptotes: both axes, x=0 and y=0. Domain: x\neq 0. Range: y\neq 0.
  • Limit sentence: x\to -\infty means x is a huge negative number. y\to 0 means y gets very close to 0.

Worked mini-examples

Example · exponential y=2^x

Quick fact: y=a^x with base a>0 and a\neq 1. Any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1, so the graph crosses the y-axis at (0,1). When x=1, y=a (the base). The asymptote is y=0. Domain: all real x. Range: y>0.

Step 1 · 2^0=1, so the y-intercept is (0,1).

Step 2 · 2^1=2, so the curve passes through (1,2). The height is the base.

Step 3 · 2^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{2} and 2^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{4}. Going left, the outputs get smaller and stay positive. The asymptote is y=0.

Step 4 · Limit sentence: as x\to -\infty, y\to 0. Going right, x\to \infty and y\to \infty. There is no ceiling on the right.

Domain: all real x, or (-\infty,\infty). Range: y>0, or (0,\infty).

Example · hyperbola y=\dfrac{1}{x}

Quick fact: y=\dfrac{k}{x} is a rectangular hyperbola. k is a fixed numerator. The graph has no value at x=0. If k>0, the two branches are in quadrants 1 and 3. If k<0, the branches are in quadrants 2 and 4. The asymptotes are x=0 and y=0.

Step 1 · x=1 gives y=1. x=-1 gives y=-1. The points are (1,1) and (-1,-1).

Step 2 · x=0 is not allowed. Mark a star there, not a point.

Step 3 · Positive divided by positive stays in quadrant 1. Positive divided by negative stays in quadrant 3. The branches are disconnected.

Step 4 · Asymptotes x=0 and y=0. Four limit sentences: as x\to \infty, y\to 0; as x\to -\infty, y\to 0; as x\to 0^+, y\to \infty; as x\to 0^-, y\to -\infty.

Dashed teal lines are the asymptotes. Solid blue is the graph. Both axes use the same scale.

Example · negative numerator y=-\dfrac{1}{x}

Quick fact: Same form y=\dfrac{k}{x}. A negative k turns every output into its opposite, so the branches move to quadrants 2 and 4. The asymptotes stay x=0 and y=0.

Same method · x=1 gives y=-1. x=-1 gives y=1.

The right branch is in quadrant 4. The left branch is in quadrant 2. The asymptotes are still both axes.

Dashed teal lines are the asymptotes. Solid blue is the graph. Both axes use the same scale.

Example · a negative exponent y=2^{-x}

Quick fact: a^{-x}=\dfrac{1}{a^{x}}. The graph of y=a^{-x} is y=a^{x} reflected in the y-axis. The y-intercept is still (0,1). The height equals the base when x=-1. The asymptote is still y=0.

Step 1 · 2^{-0}=1, so the y-intercept is still (0,1).

Step 2 · At x=-1, 2^{-(-1)}=2^1=2. The height equals the base on the left.

Step 3 · Going right, 2^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{2} and 2^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{4}. The curve falls toward y=0. This is y=2^x reflected in the y-axis.
