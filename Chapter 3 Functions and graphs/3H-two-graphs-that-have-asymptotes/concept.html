Chapter 3 · Functions and graphs

3H · Two graphs that have asymptotes

Direct path: Concept → Tutorial → Exercise.

What this section is about

  • Two families: exponential curves, y=a^x, and rectangular hyperbolas, y=\dfrac{k}{x}.
  • Asymptote: an asymptote is a line the graph gets closer to but does not cross.
  • Exponential shape: the graph stays above the x-axis. On the left it flattens toward that axis. On the right it climbs.
  • Hyperbola shape: two separate branches. A positive numerator puts them in quadrants 1 and 3. A negative numerator puts them in quadrants 2 and 4.
  • What you will write: a short table, a sketch, the asymptote equations, the domain and range, and a limit sentence such as “as x becomes huge and negative, y gets closer to 0”.

Full detail and tiny worked examples live in the Tutorial · theory. This page only sets the scene.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Keep these in mind. The Tutorial shows each one with numbers.

  • y=\dfrac{1}{x} means 1 divided by x. The same rule can be written xy=1.
  • You cannot divide by zero. When x=0, y=\dfrac{1}{x} has no value.
  • A power: 2^3=2\times 2\times 2=8. Any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1, so a^0=1.
