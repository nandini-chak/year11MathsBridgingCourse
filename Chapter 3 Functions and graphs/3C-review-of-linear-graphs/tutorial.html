Theory · key ideas

A linear graph is a straight line. Two points fix it. The easiest two points are usually the axis intercepts.

  • Gradient–intercept form: y=mx+b, or f(x)=mx+b. m is the gradient. b is the y-intercept.
  • General form: ax+by+c=0, with b\neq 0 when the graph is a function. You can always move terms so this matches y=mx+b.
  • Two intercepts: put y=0 for the x-intercept. Put x=0 for the y-intercept. Join the points.
  • Horizontal: if there is no x term, the line is y=k.
  • Vertical: if there is no y term, the line is x=\ell. This is the one straight line that is not a function.
  • Through the origin: if the constant is 0, both intercepts are (0, 0). Plot a second point, such as x=1.
  • Domain and range: a horizontal line y=k has every real x, and range just y=k. A vertical line x=k has domain just x=k, and every real y. A slanted line has every real x and every real y.

Worked mini-examples

Example · sketch y=mx+b

Quick fact: In y=mx+b, m is the gradient (change in y divided by change in x) and b is the y-intercept.

Step 1 · Use y=2x-3. Put x=0.

y=2\times 0-3=0-3=-3.

The y-intercept is (0,-3).

Step 2 · Put y=0.

0=2x-3.

Add 3 to both sides: 2x=3.

Divide by 2: x=\dfrac{3}{2}.

The x-intercept is \left(\dfrac{3}{2},0\right).

The line y = 2x − 3 crosses at (3/2, 0) and (0, −3).

Step 3 · Check the gradient from those two points. Change in y is 0-(-3)=3. Change in x is \dfrac{3}{2}-0=\dfrac{3}{2}.

m=\dfrac{3}{\dfrac{3}{2}}=3\times\dfrac{2}{3}=2.

That matches the 2 in front of x.

Example · sketch ax+by+c=0 and read a crossing

Quick fact: For a line written with every term on the left, the intercept method is the same: cover the graph by putting y=0, then x=0.

Line 1 · x+2y-6=0. Put y=0: x-6=0, so x=6. Put x=0: 2y-6=0, so y=3.

Line 2 · y=x-3. Put y=0: x=3. Put x=0: y=-3.

x+2y-6=0 y=x-3

Solid blue: x + 2y − 6 = 0. Dashed teal: y = x − 3. They meet at (4, 1).

The lines meet at (4,1). Check with algebra so the picture is not just a guess.

Call x+2y-6=0 equation (1), and write y=x-3 as x-y-3=0 for equation (2).

Subtract (2) from (1). Subtracting a bracket flips the signs inside it: (x+2y-6)-(x-y-3)=x+2y-6-x+y+3.

3y-3=0. Add 3: 3y=3. Divide by 3: y=1.

Put y=1 into (1): x+2-6=0, so x=4.

Check in (2): 4-1-3=0. The algebra agrees with the graph. The lines meet at (4,1).

Example · horizontal, vertical, and through the origin

Quick fact: The two-intercept method needs a non-zero x term, a non-zero y term, and a non-zero constant. If one of those is missing, use the special case instead.

Horizontal · y+2=0 has no x. Subtract 2: y=-2. Every point is at height −2.

Horizontal line y = −2. Domain: every real x. Range: y = −2 only.

Vertical · x-3=0 has no y. Add 3: x=3. Every point has x-coordinate 3.

Vertical line x = 3. This fails the vertical-line test, so it is not a function.

Origin · x+2y=0 has no constant, so it passes through (0, 0). Put x=1: 1+2y=0, so 2y=-1 and y=-\dfrac{1}{2}.

The line passes through (0, 0) and (1, −1/2).

The vertical line is the one that is not a function. One x-value, x = 3, is paired with every y-value, not just one.
