Chapter 3 · Functions and graphs

3C · Review of linear graphs

Direct path: Concept → Tutorial → Exercise.

What this section is about

A linear graph is a straight line. You sketch it from the gradient and from the places it crosses the axes.

  • Gradient–intercept form: y=mx+b. The gradient m is how much y changes when x grows by 1. b is the y-intercept.
  • General form: ax+by+c=0. If b is not zero, you can solve for y, and the line is a function.
  • Two-intercept sketch: put y=0 to get the x-intercept, put x=0 to get the y-intercept, then rule the line through those two points.
  • Special lines: y=k is horizontal, x=\ell is vertical (a relation, not a function), and y=mx goes through the origin.
  • Two lines meet at the one point that fits both equations. Read that point on a graph, then check it with algebra.

Full detail and tiny worked examples live in the Tutorial · theory. This page only sets the scene.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Keep these in mind. The Tutorial shows each one with numbers.

  • Gradient: from one point to another, gradient = (change in y) ÷ (change in x).
  • The y-intercept is where the line crosses the y-axis, so x=0.
  • The x-intercept is where the line crosses the x-axis, so y=0.
  • ax+by+c=0 is the same line with every term moved to the left-hand side.
  • A point is on the line when substituting its x and y makes the equation true.
