Chapter 3 · Functions and graphs

3F · The quadratic formulae and the graph

Direct path: Concept → Tutorial → Exercise.

What this section is about

Two formulae let you sketch y=ax^2+bx+c when factoring is slow.

  • Picture it: a parabola is a symmetric curve. The axis formula gives the mirror line. The quadratic formula gives the x-intercepts, when they exist.
  • Read a, b and c: in y=ax^2+bx+c, a is in front of x^2, b is in front of x, and c stands alone.
  • Axis formula: the mirror line is x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}. Put that x back into the quadratic to get the height of the vertex.
  • Discriminant: the discriminant is the number \Delta=b^2-4ac. It tells you how many zeros the quadratic has.
  • How many zeros: if \Delta>0, two zeros. If \Delta=0, one zero and the vertex sits on the x-axis. If \Delta<0, no zeros, because a negative number has no square root in this course.
  • Shape: if a>0 the parabola opens upward (a valley). If a<0 it opens downward (a hill).
  • y-intercept: the point (0,c).

Full detail and one numeric example live in the Tutorial · theory. This page only sets the scene.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Factoring and completing the square already exist. This section is the formula route.

  • You can already sketch some parabolas by factoring or by completing the square. Here you use formulae instead.
  • A square of a negative number is positive: (-3)^2=9.
  • Substitute: replace x with the number, then calculate.
  • A positive number has two square roots. The square root of 0 is 0. A negative number has no square root in this course.
  • Order: brackets, then powers, then multiply, then add.
