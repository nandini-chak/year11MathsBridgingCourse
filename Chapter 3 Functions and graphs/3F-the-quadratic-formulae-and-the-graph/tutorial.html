3F · The quadratic formulae and the graph

Short key ideas and one worked example. Read the example, then use the same steps on the Exercise.

T Teacher note
Classroom wording is a short formula route for y=ax^2+bx+c. The numeric example uses y=-x^2+6x+1. Exercise misprints are listed once on the Exercise page.

Theory · key ideas

Learn the axis formula first. Then learn the quadratic formula. The discriminant decides whether the second formula has answers.

  • a, b, c: from y=ax^2+bx+c.
  • Axis formula: x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}. This vertical line is the mirror. Substitute that x to get the vertex height.
  • Discriminant: the discriminant is the number \Delta=b^2-4ac. It tells you how many zeros the quadratic has.
  • Quadratic formula: x=\dfrac{-b+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a} and x=\dfrac{-b-\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}. Calculate \Delta before you take the square root.
  • Three cases: \Delta>0 means two zeros. \Delta=0 means one zero (the vertex is on the x-axis). \Delta<0 means no zeros.
  • Shape and intercept: a>0 opens upward, a<0 opens downward. The y-intercept is (0,c).

Worked mini-example

Example · axis, then quadratic formula

Quick fact: In y=ax^2+bx+c, a is the number in front of x^2, b is the number in front of x, and c is the number with no x. In this example a=-1, b=6 and c=1.

Worked example. Sketch y=-x^2+6x+1. Any zero that is not a whole number should be given as a simplified surd, then to three decimal places.

Quick fact: In y=ax^2+bx+c, the number a is in front of x^2. If a is positive, the parabola is concave upward: it opens up, like a valley. If a is negative, it is concave downward: it opens down, like a hill.

Here a=-1, which is negative, so this parabola opens downward.

Opens downward.

Quick fact: The y-intercept is the point where x=0. Every term with an x becomes 0, so the height is c, the number on its own.

Here c=1, so the y-intercept is (0,1).

The y-intercept is (0,1).

Formula: The axis of symmetry is x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}. In this part a=-1 and b=6.

The top of the fraction is -b=-6.

The bottom is 2a=2\times (-1)=-2.

x=\dfrac{-6}{-2}=3.

The axis of symmetry is the line x=3.

Put the axis value x=3 back into y=-x^2+6x+1. The height of that point is the vertex.

Square first: (3)^2=9.

There is a minus in front of x^2, so that term is -(9)=-9.

The x term is 6\times 3=18.

The number on its own stays 1.

y=-9+18+1=10.

The vertex is (3,10).

Formula: \Delta=b^2-4ac. The discriminant is this one number. It tells you how many zeros the quadratic has. In this part a=-1, b=6 and c=1.

b^2=(6)^2=36.

4ac=4\times (-1)\times 1=-4.

\Delta=36-(-4)=40.

\Delta=40.

\Delta=40 is greater than 0. A positive number has two square roots, one plus and one minus.

There are two x-intercepts (two zeros).

Formula: The zeros are x=\dfrac{-b+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a} and x=\dfrac{-b-\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}, where \Delta=b^2-4ac. In this part a=-1, b=6, c=1 and \Delta=40.

The top starts with -b=-6.

The bottom is 2a=-2.

Take out the square factor: \sqrt{40}=\sqrt{4\times 10}=2\sqrt{10}.

The two tops are -6+2\sqrt{10} and -6-2\sqrt{10}. Both sit over -2.

x=\dfrac{-6+2\sqrt{10}}{-2} or x=\dfrac{-6-2\sqrt{10}}{-2}.

Divide each piece of -6+2\sqrt{10} by -2.

\dfrac{-6}{-2}=3.

\dfrac{2\sqrt{10}}{-2}=-\sqrt{10}.

Add those two results: x=3-\sqrt{10}.

Divide each piece of -6-2\sqrt{10} by -2.

\dfrac{-6}{-2}=3.

\dfrac{-2\sqrt{10}}{-2}=\sqrt{10}.

Add those two results: x=3+\sqrt{10}.

The zeros are x=3-\sqrt{10} and x=3+\sqrt{10}.

Now approximate each zero correct to 3 decimal places. Look at the first digit after the ones you keep. If it is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, leave the last digit. If it is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, add 1 to the last digit.

Zero 3-\sqrt{10}: the calculator value starts -0.16228. To 3 decimal places that is -0.162.

Zero 3+\sqrt{10}: the calculator value starts 6.16228. To 3 decimal places that is 6.162.

To 3 decimal places the zeros are -0.162 and 6.162.

Plot these points, then draw a smooth parabola through them: (3, 10), (0, 1), 3−√10, 3+√10.

If the discriminant had been negative, you would stop after that calculation. There would be no x-intercepts. You would still plot the y-intercept, the vertex, and one mirror point.
