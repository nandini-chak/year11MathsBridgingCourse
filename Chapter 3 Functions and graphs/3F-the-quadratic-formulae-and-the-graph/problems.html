14 · Exercise 3F Challenge

Let f(x)=ax^2+bx+c, with a\neq 0.

(a) Show that f\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)=f(0).

(b) Explain part (a) using the symmetry of the parabola.

Let f(x)=ax^2+bx+c, with a\neq 0. We need f\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)=f(0).

First, f(0)=c, because every term with x is 0 and the constant is left.

Now replace x with -\dfrac{b}{a}.

f\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)=a\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^2+b\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)+c.

Square first: \left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)^2=\dfrac{b^2}{a^2}.

Multiply by a: a\times\dfrac{b^2}{a^2}=\dfrac{b^2}{a}.

The middle term: b\times\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)=-\dfrac{b^2}{a}.

\dfrac{b^2}{a}+\left(-\dfrac{b^2}{a}\right)+c=c.

Both sides equal c, so f\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)=f(0).

Quick fact: The axis of y=ax^2+bx+c is the mirror line x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}.

The two inputs in part (a) are x=0 and x=-\dfrac{b}{a}.

The point halfway between them is \dfrac{0+\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)}{2}=-\dfrac{b}{2a}.

That halfway value is exactly the axis. So x=0 and x=-\dfrac{b}{a} are the same distance from the mirror, on opposite sides.

A mirror keeps the height. Points the same distance from the axis have the same y-value.

That is why f(0)=f\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right).
