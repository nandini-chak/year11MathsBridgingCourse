Chapter 3 · Functions and graphs

3G · Powers, cubics, and circles

Direct path: Concept → Tutorial → Exercise.

What this section is about

Three pictures: a power of x, a cubic, and a circle centred at the origin.

  • Even power. The index is 2, 4, 6, and so on. Then (-x)^n=x^n. Example: (-2)^4=16 and 2^4=16. The graph of y=x^n is a mirror copy across the y-axis.
  • Odd power. The index is 1, 3, 5, and so on. Then (-x)^n=-x^n. Example: (-2)^3=-8 and 2^3=8. A half-turn about the origin leaves the graph sitting on itself.
  • Circle equation. x^2+y^2=r^2 means every point is the same distance r from (0,0). That distance is the radius. The centre is the origin.
  • Cubic intercepts. If a cubic is written as a product of factors, the graph meets the x-axis where any factor is zero. The y-intercept is the height when x=0.

Full detail and tiny worked examples live in the Tutorial · theory. This page only sets the scene.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Keep these in mind. The Tutorial shows each one with numbers.

  • Indices: x^3 means x\times x\times x. x^4 means x\times x\times x\times x.
  • Square roots: \sqrt{9}=3 because 3\times 3=9. The symbol \sqrt{\ } means the zero or positive root.
  • Plotting: from the origin, move across to the x-value, then up or down to the y-value. Plot a few points, then join them with a smooth curve.
