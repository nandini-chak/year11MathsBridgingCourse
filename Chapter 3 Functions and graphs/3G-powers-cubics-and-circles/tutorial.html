Tutorial · theory

3G · Powers, cubics, and circles

Short key ideas and three tiny worked examples. Read one example, then try the same steps on the Exercise.

Theory · key ideas

Plot a few points, then join them smoothly. The words even power, odd power, circle equation, and cubic intercept are defined on the Concept page.

  • Power y=x^n: try x between 0 and 1, and x bigger than 1. A higher index is flatter between -1 and 1, and steeper outside.
  • Odd index (1, 3, 5, …): the curve passes through (0,0). For 3, 5, 7, … it is flat there for a moment, then it climbs. A half-turn about the origin leaves it unchanged.
  • Even index (2, 4, 6, …): the curve stays on or above the x-axis and turns at the origin. It is a mirror in the y-axis.
  • y=\sqrt{x}: only x\ge 0, and only the zero or positive answer. It is the top half of the sideways parabola y^2=x. It leaves the origin vertically. Domain x\ge 0. Range y\ge 0.
  • Factored cubic: mark a dot where each factor is zero. The y-intercept is the height at x=0. Test one number in each gap and write the sign. A squared factor touches the axis and turns back. Do not mark the turning points. We cannot find those yet.
  • Circle x^2+y^2=r^2: centre (0,0), radius r. Mark (\pm r,0) and (0,\pm r). Domain and range both run from -r to r. A full circle is not a function: a vertical line can hit it twice.
  • Four halves: upper y=\sqrt{r^2-x^2}, lower y=-\sqrt{r^2-x^2}, right x=\sqrt{r^2-y^2}, left x=-\sqrt{r^2-y^2}.

Worked mini-examples

Example · a power graph

Quick fact: If the index is even, (-x)^n=x^n (mirror in the y-axis). If the index is odd, (-x)^n=-x^n (a half-turn about the origin leaves the graph unchanged).

Step 1 · y=x^3 is an odd power. (-1)^3=-1 and 1^3=1, so the points (-1,-1) and (1,1) are a half-turn apart.

Step 2 · Near the origin the output is tiny. (0.5)^3=0.5\times 0.5\times 0.5=0.125. The height 0.125 is much smaller than the input 0.5, so the curve is flat at (0,0).

Step 3 · Join the points smoothly. The curve runs from the lower left, through the origin, into the upper right.

Example · a cubic

Quick fact: A product is zero when any factor is zero. Those x-values are the x-intercepts. The y-intercept is the value at x=0.

Step 1 · y=(x+1)(x-2)(x-4). Set each factor to zero: x=-1, x=2, x=4.

Step 2 · Height on the y-axis: y=(0+1)(0-2)(0-4)=(1)(-2)(-4)=8. Mark (0,8).

Step 3 · Test one x in each gap. x=-2 gives (-1)(-4)(-6)=-24, so the curve is below the axis on the left. x=0 gives positive 8. x=3 gives (4)(1)(-1)=-4, so negative. x=5 gives (6)(3)(1)=18, so positive.

Step 4 · Sketch from below, cross at -1, stay above through (0,8), cross at 2, stay below, cross at 4, then rise. Do not mark the turning points.

Example · a circle

Formula: x^2+y^2=r^2 has centre (0,0) and radius r. The radius is the square root of the number on the right.

Step 1 · x^2+y^2=4. r=\sqrt{4}=2, because 2\times 2=4.

Step 2 · On the axes, one coordinate is 0. y=0 gives x=\pm 2. x=0 gives y=\pm 2.

Step 3 · Domain -2\le x\le 2. Range -2\le y\le 2. The full circle is not a function.
