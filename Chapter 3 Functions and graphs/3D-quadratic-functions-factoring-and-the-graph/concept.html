Chapter 3 · Functions and graphs

3D · Quadratic functions — factoring and the graph

Direct path: Concept → Tutorial → Exercise.

What this section is about

A quadratic graph is a parabola. Before you sketch it, answer the same five questions, in the same order, every time.

  • The shape of the rule: y=ax^2+bx+c with a\neq 0. If a=1, the quadratic is called monic.
  • Five questions, in this order: which way up, y-intercept, zeroes, axis of symmetry, vertex.
  • Which way up: positive a opens up (holds water). Negative a opens down.
  • y-intercept: put x=0. For y=ax^2+bx+c that height is c.
  • Zeroes: the x-intercepts. If the rule factors as a(x-r)(x-s), the zeroes are x=r and x=s. A root is the same number, read as the solution of the equation y=0.
  • Axis: the vertical line halfway between the zeroes. Add the zeroes and divide by 2.
  • Vertex: the turning point. It sits on the axis. Put that x back into the rule to get the height.
  • A perfect square such as (x-1)^2 touches the x-axis in one place. That is a double zero, also called equal zeroes. Two different zeroes are called distinct.
  • Same zeroes, different steepness: y=a(x-r)(x-s). Changing a changes how steep the curve is. The sign of a flips it up or down.

Worked numbers and sketches are in the Tutorial. This page only sets the scene.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Keep these in mind. The Tutorial uses each one with numbers.

  • Expand brackets: (x+1)(x-3)=x^2-2x-3.
  • Factor a monic quadratic: find two numbers that multiply to the constant and add to the middle number.
  • Average of two zeroes: add them, then divide by 2.
  • If A\times B=0, then A=0 or B=0.
