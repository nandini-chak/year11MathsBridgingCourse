3E · Completing the square and the graph

Short key ideas and tiny worked examples. Read one example, then try the same steps on the Exercise.

Theory · key ideas

A completed square is a perfect square plus a leftover constant. The vertex sits where that square is zero.

  • Monic: middle number, halve, square, add and subtract.
  • Non-monic: factor the front number out first. The inside then starts with x^2.
  • Vertex form: y=a(x-h)^2+k turns at (h,k). The axis is the vertical line x=h.
  • Square fact: (x-h)^2 is never negative, so the curve does not go past the vertex.
  • Zeroes: put y=0. If the square would have to be negative, there is no x-intercept.

Worked mini-examples

Example · monic, whole-number half

Quick fact: For x^2+bx+c, halve b, square the half, then add and subtract that square.

Step 1 · f(x)=x^2+6x+5. The middle number is 6.

Step 2 · Halve 6: 6\div 2=3.

Step 3 · Square the half: 3^2=9.

Step 4 · Add and subtract 9, then group.

f(x)=x^2+6x+5=(x^2+6x+9)-9+5=(x+3)^2-4

Check the constants: -9+5=-4. The bracket x^2+6x+9 is (x+3)^2.

Example · monic, negative middle number

Same steps. Halve, square, add and subtract. A negative half still squares to a positive number.

Step 1 · f(x)=x^2-4x-5. The middle number is -4.

Step 2 · Halve: -4\div 2=-2.

Step 3 · Square: (-2)^2=4.

Step 4 · Add and subtract 4.

f(x)=x^2-4x-5=(x^2-4x+4)-4-5=(x-2)^2-9

Example · non-monic, factor the front number out

Quick fact: If x^2 is multiplied by something other than 1, factor that number out first. Then use the monic steps on the inside.

Step 1 · y=2x^2-12x+16. The front number is 2. Factor 2 out of every term.

y=2x^2-12x+16=2(x^2-6x+8)

Step 2 · Inside, the middle number is -6. Halve: -3. Square: (-3)^2=9.

Step 3 · Add and subtract 9 inside the bracket.

x^2-6x+8=(x^2-6x+9)-9+8=(x-3)^2-1

Step 4 · Multiply by 2 again. Two times the square stays a square term. Two times -1 is -2.

y=2\left((x-3)^2-1\right)=2(x-3)^2-2

Example · read the vertex, then the zeroes

Quick fact: y=a(x-h)^2+k has axis x=h and vertex (h,k).

Quick fact: A square is never negative. 2(x-3)^2 is 0 at x=3, and positive for every other x. So y is never below -2.

Step 1 · From the example above, y=2(x-3)^2-2. The bracket is x-3, so h=3. The added number is k=-2.

Step 2 · Axis x=3. Vertex (3,-2). The front number 2 is positive, so the arms open upward.

Step 3 · Zeroes: put y=0.

2(x-3)^2-2=0

2(x-3)^2=2

(x-3)^2=1

A square equals 1 when the inside is 1 or when the inside is -1.

x-3=1, so x=4. Or x-3=-1, so x=2.

The dot on the graph is the vertex. Its label sits off the curve.

Same method with a negative front number: y=-x^2+8x-15=-(x^2-8x+15). Inside, half of -8 is -4, and (-4)^2=16.

x^2-8x+15=(x-4)^2-1, so y=-(x-4)^2+1.

Here h=4 and k=1. The front number is negative, so the arms open downward and the curve never goes above y=1. Putting y=0 gives x=5 or x=3.

If the square would have to equal a negative number, there is no x-intercept. If the square equals 0, there is one x-intercept and the vertex is on the x-axis.
